Thales to Aristotle vocabulary:

  1. Argument - units of reasoning, as observed by Aristotle when systematically investigating the way his predecessors thought about the world and reached conclusions.
  2. Statements are parts of an argument
    • Premise(s)
    • Conclusion
  3. Deductive Arguments
    • Valid - conclusion follows necessarily from the premise(s)
    • Sound - argument is valid and the premise(s) are true

Sound premises, but false conclusion (invalid argument)

            
                All modern domestic cats are direct descendants of the African wildcat
                African wildcats sit and wait for prey to come within striking distance
                Therefore domestic cats patiently wait to be fed
            
            Domestic cat food is not prey, nor is there necessarily any evidence (or likelihood) that African wildcats are patient as they wait, therefore the conclusion does not follow the premises.
            Is this an example of an invalid inference?

Modus ponens: sufficient condition - mode of affirming 

            if p, then q.
                       p
                       ∴ q
        

            if that's a cat, it's a mammal.
            it is a cat,
            ∴ it is a mammal
Modus tollens: necessary condition - mode of denying 

            if p, then q.
                       not q
                       ∴ p
        

            Citizens of a democracy have the right to vote
            The save act makes voting impossible for some citizens
            Therefore, the save act makes a government no longer a democracy
Fallacy of denying the antecedent
  • Antecedent: if

            if p, then q.
                       not p
                       ∴ not q
        

            if a latte, then a coffee drink
            A cold brew is not a latte
            ∴ a cold brew is not a coffee drink
Fallacy of affirming the consequent
  • Consequent: then

            if p, then q.
                       q
                       ∴ p
        

            If a latte is a coffee drink
            and a cold brew is a coffee drink
            A cold brew is a latte
Vocabulary
  • Philosophy (Greek etymology: love of wisdom): Investigation of the fundamental nature of reality, its concepts and principles
  • Ethics:
    • I do not have an adequate definition for ethics. Dr Brenner's Logic & Philosophy (p.8 -> value is key concept & glossary -> moral philosophy) offers definitions that intersect, but do not nearly encompass how ethics is commonly used (except for 'moral' philosophy, which I address below).
    • Value: the worth of something. Imagine a beautiful shirt, light and breathable cloth with blues and greens invoking a magical forest, having tiny stitches and easy to care for. That shirt would surely be valuable. But what if it was made by kids in a crowded room, underfed, working 80 hour days with mean adults supervising. How could that be ethically right?
    • Moral philosophy: but isn't morality a construct made by society. So many horrific crimes throughout history are committed based the 'moral' value of some people over others: differing religions, countries, sects, cultures, wealth levels, colors, sex ... Since I do not want to go on a rant, I will not give examples, but certainly moral values change by culture, time and place.
    Also the gleanings, raise more questions in my head than answers about what the word ethics actually means). Back in early October, 2025, I tried to consolidate my understanding of the ethical part of the gleanings into a coherent couple of paragraphs to send to you (Dr Brenner) before my visit, but it was too hard for the amount of time I had. What you said in logic class back in the late 70s rang true for me: the best argument against abortion is a picture of the fetus. The best argument for abortion, in my opinion, would be the life stories and statistics of unhappy, unwanted children (who are statistically more likely to be criminals or drug users) and their financially unstable, desperate, single mothers. The common thread is emotion. Basically ethics seems best described with examples, emotional reactions and the ability to empathize. Emotions can be strong and shared. For example, over 8 million people in the US demonstrated in favor of KINDNESS on March 28, 2026 (initiated by US Americans, but in addition, includes people from every continent).
  • Epistemology: investigation of the fundamental principles of knowledge. Is knowledge understanding? But understanding is generally wrong or incomplete. The principles of knowledge vary based on activity, for example the scientific method or how to paint a house. In philosophy, are the fundamental principles of knowledge methods of finding fundamental truth? Is there even such a thing?
  • Metaphysics: is the investigation of the fundamental principles of reality or being. My personal example comes from my teenage years. A good friend of mine spent a lot of time struggling with the belief that our universe could never have been absolute nothingness, because how could the world we know have developed from nothing; How can anything exist without a beginning? I do not know if he ever reached a conclusion.
  • Logic (logos: speech, reason): is reasoning about reasoning itself, a study of how to create a valid conclusion given the truth of the preceding statements, in other words, valid inferences.
