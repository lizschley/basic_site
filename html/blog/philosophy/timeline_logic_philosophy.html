    1. Introduction
Sixth century B.C.
  • Thales of Miletus (born c. 624–620 bce—died c. 548–545 bce): everything is made of hydor (water)
  • Anaximander (born 610 bce, Miletus [now in Turkey]—died 546 bce): No fire is wet and cool, water is essentially wet and cool, therefore file is NOT made of water
  • Thales and Anaximander - initiated tradition of systematic reasoning about fundamental principles of the natural world
  • Parmenides (born c. 515 BCE) was a Greek philosopher of Elea in southern Italy who founded Eleaticism, one of the leading pre-Socratic schools of Greek thought.
Fifth century B.C.
  • Democritus (born c. 460 bce—died c. 370): atomism, putting aside conventions (ie.hot & cold, bitter & sour, colors, in reality there are only atoms and the void
  • Socrates (born c. 470 bce, Athens—died 399 bce, Athens): systematic reasoning about moral reasoning, proposed this moral reasoning was radically different than principles of the natural world
Fourth century B.C.
  • Plato (born 428/427 bce, Athens, Greece—died 348/347, Athens): student of Socrates (above) and teacher of Aristotle (see below): made Socrates famous by writing his dialogs. Plato also founded the Academy (first university.)
  • Aristotle (born 384 bce, Stagira, Chalcidice, Greece—died 322, Chalcis, Euboea): invented Logic, systematic investigation of the principles of reasoning. He did this by looking back at the arguments (units of reasoning) of his predecessors from Thales to Plato and recognizing their thought patterns
